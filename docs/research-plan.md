# Turn assumptions into useful evidence

Start with a wearing situation, then check whether people actually experience the problem. The activities below are proposed; no interviews, consumer tests, or garment tests are reported as completed.

## 1. Tighten the desk research

Use the [landscape](./competitive-landscape.md) to select a small comparison set: an accessible basic, a style-led tee, a premium staple, and a blank supplier. Compare the specific products a shopper could realistically substitute for one another.

For each product, record:

- Exact product and white color variant, source URL, date, market, and currency.
- Regular and promotional price separately; note pack count and calculate price per shirt only from a verified pack price.
- Brand-stated fabric, weight, fit, origin, and care details, each attached to its source.
- The buying question the page answers well and what information is difficult to find.
- Missing or inaccessible evidence, rather than filling gaps with estimates.

Include the shopper's existing shirt as a substitute. “Buy nothing” is a useful comparison when judging whether the proposed reason to buy is meaningful.

**Output:** a dated evidence table. Product-page claims establish what a brand says, not independent performance or market share.

## 2. Hear recent purchase stories

Recruit a small convenience sample of people who recently bought or considered a white tee in the proposed situation. Five or six conversations can help expose missing questions for an early prototype; they do not represent the entire market or establish market size.

Use a short conversation guide:

1. “Tell me about the last time you looked for a white T-shirt.”
2. “Where were you planning to wear it?”
3. “What alternatives did you consider, including something you already owned?”
4. “What did you need to know before deciding?”
5. “What happened after you wore or washed it?”

Ask follow-ups about actual events. Avoid opening with the concept pitch or asking whether someone would buy a hypothetical “perfect” shirt. With permission, keep brief notes without unnecessary identifying details; obtain separate permission before using a quote publicly.

**Output:** a short pattern summary with examples, contradictions, and participant count. Label interpretation separately from what people actually said.

## 3. Select one brief

Review the [three ideas](./ideas.md) against the stories. Choose one primary need and a contrasting secondary need. Write a promise that the page and the available product evidence can support.

Record the decision and its reason. Keep the name, archetype, voice, and visual direction provisional until they serve the selected audience situation. Do not turn an AI recommendation into a recorded user choice.

**Output:** two small persona hypotheses and a compact early brief. Mark unresolved product facts as unknown.

## 4. Compare two controlled hero studies

Once a brief is selected, make one modernist and one postmodernist study using inspected primary design references. Keep the shirt, headline, supporting copy, action, and any product facts identical. Compare layout, hierarchy, typography, and image treatment.

Show the studies in varied order across participants. Ask what each page seems to offer, who it appears to address, and where they would act next. Give a concrete task instead of relying only on a favorite-design vote. Check desktop and narrow layouts, keyboard access, readability, and whether research labels are clear.

**Output:** observed confusions and an explained direction choice. This exploratory comparison is not a conversion experiment or statistically representative preference test. The method follows the [source exercise's controlled comparison](https://github.com/kaw393939/is117-brand-studio/blob/ca4b26431310a419e7143c56cacbc450c452879b/AGENTS.md#3-compare-two-annotated-hero-directions).

## 5. Verify product claims only when there is a real sample

If the project remains a fictional storefront, omit unsupported performance claims. If actual procurement is being considered, first agree on a sample budget and a small comparable set. Buying samples is a later decision.

| Question | Possible observation | What it would not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does the garment change size after washing? | Measure the same marked locations before and after a documented care-label wash and drying process. Repeat where feasible. | A universal shrinkage claim across production batches. |
| How does the fabric appear in use? | Photograph the same garment setup under consistent light and background conditions. Record the limitations. | A standardized opacity rating or a guarantee on all bodies and skin tones. |
| What is the fit like? | Record garment measurements and consenting wearers' observations with the selected size and context. | A fit promise for every buyer. |
| What happens to the collar or seams? | Document visible condition before and after a specified number of wear/wash cycles. | Long-term durability or superiority without adequate comparative testing. |

Keep dated notes, measurements, conditions, and unedited comparison images locally in `references/`. Publish only an appropriate summary and permitted evidence after review. Care-label checks and small sample observations are limited evidence, not laboratory certification.

## 6. Build one useful slice, then review it

Apply the selected brief to the storefront and shared live style guide. Explain how two truthful persuasion elements help the visitor. Run the storefront's appropriate checks and inspect the actual rendered result. A passing automated check does not establish customer demand or a coherent brand by itself.

Record actual commands and outcomes separately from the user's browser observations. Use the reference project's [SPEC format](https://github.com/kaw393939/is117-brand-studio/blob/ca4b26431310a419e7143c56cacbc450c452879b/SPEC.md) when that implementation begins.

**First action:** validate the recommended everyday-decision situation through recent purchase stories. Revisit the direction if the evidence points elsewhere.
