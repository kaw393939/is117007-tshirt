# Three directions to investigate

These are AI-proposed hypotheses, not selected brands or findings about customers. The same plain white shirt stays fixed; each direction changes the situation, message, and information the page prioritizes.

**Recommendation:** test the everyday-decision direction first. It gives us a concrete question to investigate and a useful website behavior to prototype. This is a research priority, not a decision made for the user.

## 1. An easier everyday decision

**Primary situation:** someone choosing a simple outfit for a day that moves between class, work, and ordinary errands. **Secondary check:** someone who wants the shirt mainly as an underlayer and needs different fit information.

| Element | Working hypothesis |
| --- | --- |
| Unmet question | “Can I understand how this will work in my day before I buy?” |
| Draft promise | “A clear starting point for getting dressed.” |
| Meaning | Confidence through useful information and an uncomplicated choice. |
| Possible brand character | Sage: helpful, specific, calm. This describes the brand's voice, not a diagnosis of the buyer. |
| Page emphasis | A readable product overview, relevant fit information where verified, outfit context, and a clear next action. |
| Evidence needed | Recent purchase stories, the questions people could not answer, and an observed task with a prototype. Verify any fit or fabric claim separately. |
| Main risk | Generic minimalism. A calm layout alone does not create a distinctive reason to choose the shirt. |

**First test:** interview people about their last white-shirt purchase. Look for decision uncertainty in their actual stories before showing this concept. Then ask them to find information needed for a specific outfit using a prototype.

**Truthful persuasion candidates:** freely available outfit guidance may provide value through reciprocity. An optional choice of a wearing situation followed by relevant guidance could support consistency if the later action follows a voluntary prior commitment. Required size selection alone would not establish that mechanism.

## 2. A blank canvas for personal style

**Primary situation:** someone building an outfit around their own accessories, layers, or styling choices. **Secondary check:** someone who prefers a single, easy-to-repeat outfit and could find endless options tiring.

| Element | Working hypothesis |
| --- | --- |
| Unmet question | “How can something this plain still feel like me?” |
| Draft promise | “One white tee. Room for your point of view.” |
| Meaning | Personal authorship rather than a visible brand badge. |
| Possible brand character | Creator: curious, expressive, encouraging. |
| Page emphasis | A few clearly labeled styling studies using the same shirt, with optional exploration and an easy route back to the product. |
| Evidence needed | Actual styling routines, examples people already use, and observations of whether the studies inspire or distract. |
| Main risk | The creative direction overwhelms the product or implies that the shirt includes customization services. |

**First test:** ask participants to show how they styled a recent basic garment. Compare whether a short styling sequence helps them describe a relevant use for the shirt. Keep product specifications unchanged.

**Truthful persuasion candidates:** offer a useful styling worksheet without requiring an email address. Use inclusive, recognizable situations to explore liking; do not imply community membership, endorsements, or customer participation that does not exist.

## 3. A considered, repeatable wardrobe

**Primary situation:** someone reducing the effort of maintaining a small rotation of everyday clothes. **Secondary check:** someone buying an occasional low-cost replacement who may not value an extended product explanation.

| Element | Working hypothesis |
| --- | --- |
| Unmet question | “What would make this a sensible shirt to choose again?” |
| Draft promise | “A simpler way to think about your everyday rotation.” |
| Meaning | Deliberate choices and less decision effort. |
| Possible brand character | Everyperson: straightforward, familiar, practical. |
| Page emphasis | Clear buying information, a modest wardrobe-planning guide, and verified care instructions if available. |
| Evidence needed | Actual repeat-purchase behavior, replacement triggers, care habits, and product measurements or sample tests before performance claims. |
| Main risk | Sliding into unsupported durability, cost-per-wear, sustainability, or “buy less” claims. |

**First test:** discuss when and why participants replaced their last shirt. Check whether a concise rotation guide answers a real need or adds unwanted work. A planning prototype does not establish longevity or environmental benefit.

**Truthful persuasion candidates:** provide care or wardrobe guidance supported by appropriate evidence. If a person voluntarily chooses a rotation goal, an optional next step related to that goal may express consistency; do not preselect enrollment, subscriptions, or multipacks.

## Choose based on evidence

| What we learn | What to do next |
| --- | --- |
| Repeated uncertainty about wearing context | Develop direction 1 and make information easier to evaluate. |
| People already use basics as a deliberate styling tool | Explore direction 2 with a short set of relevant examples. |
| Repeat buying and replacement are the central concern | Investigate direction 3, with stronger product evidence before making claims. |
| None of these situations matters in recent purchase stories | Revise the audience hypothesis before making a logo or full storefront. |

These are decision rules for discussion, not statistical thresholds or validated outcomes. After selecting a brief, compare modernist and postmodernist hero treatments with identical copy and product facts, as required by the [reference exercise](https://github.com/kaw393939/is117-brand-studio/blob/ca4b26431310a419e7143c56cacbc450c452879b/README.md#work-one-decision-at-a-time).

The persuasion proposals follow the reference project's [truthful persuasion guidance](https://github.com/kaw393939/is117-brand-studio/blob/ca4b26431310a419e7143c56cacbc450c452879b/docs/reference.md#persuasion-that-respects-a-visitor). No conversion effect has been measured.
